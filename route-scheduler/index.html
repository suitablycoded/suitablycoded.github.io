        How the plan is worked out

        • Travel time is the straight-line distance × (road factor) at an average . No real roads are used.
        • A job may start once its window opens and must finish before its window closes.
        • Each technician starts and ends the day at their depot and must be back by the end of their hours.
        • Lunch is a fixed 30-minute break. No travel or work runs across it.
        • Auto-schedule places urgent and tight-window jobs first, each in the cheapest place it fits. It then improves the plan: reversing stretches of a route, moving one to three jobs, swapping jobs between technicians, and moving a placed job to make room for one left over. Last, it runs a fixed number of rounds that take out a few jobs and put them back in a different order. When not every job fits, High jobs come before any Normal job and Normal before any Low; then it keeps the plan with fewest rule breaks, then least travel. The same jobs always give the same plan.
        • The manual plan gives each job to the technician whose depot is nearest and who has the skill, in time-window order, with no check on clashes or hours.
